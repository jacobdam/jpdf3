  - Moved `Limits` into `jpdf-traits`; `jpdf-syntax` re-exports it (ADR 0004).
  - Added decode limits and `DecodeLimits`.
  - Added a hand-written `BackendError`, because thiserror is a proc-macro and hasn't been

  - Added the traits Inflate/Deflate, Jpeg/Jpeg2000/Jbig2/Ccitt decoders, Hasher/HashState

  - Wrote spec specs/core/backend-traits.md and updated TRACE R1/R9.
  - Opened discovery #57 (JPX mixed bit depths).
- Result:
  - Local checks: fmt and clippy clean, 117/117 tests, check-specs ok, judge policy PASS.
  - Review found 5 major issues. The suites didn't enforce three claims the spec made:
    allocation before checking, lenient truncation, and hash known answers strong enough
    to catch a hand-written implementation. `DecodedImage` also lacked the Adobe APP14 flag
    and the JPX bit depth. There were also 5 minor and 2 nit findings. All fixed:
    - 65 hash vectors and RFC 6229 RC4 vectors;
    - a salvage rule;
    - a 1 GiB fixed-Huffman bomb;
    - `source_bits` and `adobe_app14`;
    - required landscape and portrait fixtures;
    - a padding probe;
    - a decryption cap.
  - Delta review: approve, with 3 minor and 2 nits. All fixed, except mixed JPX depths,
    which is now #57 and a known divergence in the spec.
- Cost: about 85% of context; 1 review round plus a delta review; 0 CI rounds so far.
- Lesson: when a conformance suite is tested only against toy implementations, the toys
  pass because they were written to the suite. Ask, for each check, "what broken adapter
  still passes?" Known answers must reach multi-block and padding-boundary paths. "Must
  salvage" rules need a positive check, not just "error or prefix". A spec claim like
  "check before allocating" must say what the suite can't observe.
