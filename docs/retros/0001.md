## What went well
- **Throughput:** 26 agent PRs in about 33 h of wall time, and every one of the 27 sessions exited cleanly (rc=0).
- **No product regressions on main:** 0 reverts and 0 code-caused red runs on main. Both main failures after bootstrap
  were Pages infra, and both were diagnosed without a code change.
- **Reviews catch real defects:** the audit found **0 critical** issues in the hostile-input core; earlier review
  rounds had already removed the panics. The 11 multi-round PRs mostly reflect real findings, not churn.
- **Lessons get captured:** 36 entries, each linked to a journal. Several were acted on later (for example, a
  `--test-package` entry from the audit).

## What went badly
- **Audits and retros starve:** the first audit ran after **26** merges instead of 10. It only happened because two
  sessions overrode TICK §2 by hand. With a non-empty backlog (22 `ready` issues), rule g always wins over i and j.
- **High rework:** **58%** of substantive PRs needed more than one fix round; the worst took 6 (#24).
- **Oversized sessions:** **42%** of substantive PRs went past TICK's own 60% context guideline, and one reached 85%
  (#56). The guideline wasn't applied when issues were sized; jpdf-traits was split into 4 parts only after part 1 grew.
- **Checkers that accept broken implementations** recurred in 3 PRs, yet the audit still found 5 more. The lesson
  existed in LESSONS.md from PR #56 onward and still didn't stop it.
- **Measurement gap:** `cargo xtask metrics` doesn't exist and journals don't record findings by category, so this
  retro's rework numbers are hand-counted.

## Root causes (5 whys)

**1. Audits/retros starve.** Why did the audit run after 26 merges? Rule g (a ready issue) always applied first. Why
was g always applicable? The M0 backlog had about 20 issues, and every review spawns discoveries. Why didn't the
due audit win? TICK §2 is a strict order with i and j last; it was written as if triggers would be rare and the
backlog would often be empty. Why didn't anyone notice sooner? Orient has no command that shows "audit due", so each
session had to work it out. **Root:** the priority order puts time-triggered work after an inexhaustible queue, and
nothing checks the trigger mechanically.

**2. High rework.** Why did 11/19 PRs need several rounds? The reviewer found real defects: unchecked arithmetic,
suites that pass broken toys, untestable journey steps. Why didn't the author catch them? The author checks against the
spec, and the spec template asks only "what could leak or be bypassed". Why didn't LESSONS.md prevent repeats? It is a
36-entry list read once at the start of the session, while these mistakes happen hours later while writing a suite.
**Root:** the lessons live in a long list instead of in the artifact being filled in at the moment of writing (the
spec template) and in the reviewer prompt.

**3. Oversized sessions.** Why did 8/19 PRs exceed 60% of context? The PRs were 2–7.6k lines: whole crates or trait
families with their suites. Why were the issues that big? The M0 backlog was written from PLAN §8 rows, one issue per
row. Why wasn't the work split when claimed? TICK says "split if it won't fit", but there is no concrete size
measure, and the cost shows up only after the review round. **Root:** there is no sizing rule that can be checked
before claiming. Review and fix rounds on big diffs then compound the context use.
